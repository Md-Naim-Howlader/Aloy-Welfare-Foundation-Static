The Challenge: Hunger & School Dropouts in Bangladesh

In Bangladesh's urban slums and impoverished rural belts, millions of children wake up not to textbooks, but to the crushing reality of hunger and child labor. Poverty forces parents to send their children to scrap-picking, hawking, or small workshop labor instead of schools.

Without a reliable source of nutrition, even children who enroll soon drop out due to severe malnutrition, chronic fatigue, and cognitive lag. Aloy Welfare Foundation recognized that to break the generational cycle of poverty, food security and educational support must go hand-in-hand.

Our 4-Pillar Comprehensive Intervention

Our field model combines daily nutrition with institutional schooling and life skills, addressing both the physical and intellectual development of each enrolled child:

1. Nutritious Daily Hot Meals

Every school day, students receive a balanced hot lunch—comprising rice, protein-rich lentils, boiled eggs, fresh vegetables, and seasonal fruit. This eliminates hunger and boosts concentration.

2. Free Books, Kits & Uniforms

Every registered student is equipped with national curriculum textbooks, notebooks, geometry kits, school bags, and durable uniforms so they never feel inferior to mainstream peers.

3. Remedial Coaching & Care

Trained volunteer mentors offer evening tutoring sessions, helping first-generation learners conquer mathematics, English, and basic ICT skills with kindness and patient guidance.

4. Health Checks & Sanitation

Regular bi-monthly health screenings, deworming medicine distributions, clean drinking water access, and dental hygiene kits ensure healthy growing bodies.
